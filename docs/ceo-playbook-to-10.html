GraniteOS — CEO Playbook: The Road to 10/10

A founder-CEO memo. Not analysis — decisions. What we stop doing, what we build in what order, how we charge, and what "10/10" actually requires.

Pranav Patil, CEO · GraniteOS · 2026-06-10

The CEO diagnosis in one line: We do not have a product problem. We have a distribution, dependency, and monetization problem. The product is a genuine 6 — but it's invisible to buyers, it can't pull users back on its own, and it doesn't charge anyone. Fix those three and the same product is a 9. The 10th point is traction, and traction can't be coded — it's earned.

1 · The strategic call (read this twice)

We will NOT become a manufacturing ERP. We will become the undisputed operating system of the Indian stone trade.

Every instinct after reading the panel ("SAP has procurement, NetSuite has accounting, Odoo has MRP…") pulls us toward breadth. That is how we die. Competing horizontally against SAP/Odoo/Zoho is a war we lose on day one. Our 10/10 is not "more modules than Odoo." Our 10/10 is: a granite/marble business cannot run a single day without us.

2 · The five problems → the five solutions

1We are invisible (GTM = 2.5/10)

Problem: The front door is a login wall. ~100% of cold traffic dies. Our own newbiz template mandates a "Contact + Get a Demo" funnel — and it's missing.

Solution — ship the shopfront (Week 1–2):

  • Public landing → features → pricing → "Book a Demo" funnel in front of the login. We already have the assets (the /showcase page, 3D, Recovery Radar) — they're just hidden.
  • Lead with the wedge, not features: "Stop losing ₹ lakhs in wasted slab. See exactly which block and machine bleeds money."
  • Free public catalog as the viral loop: every dealer who shares a GraniteOS catalog link is free distribution. Brand the link. Add "Powered by GraniteOS — get yours."
  • Trust signals: pilot logos, an ROI number, a founder face, an address, DPDP/privacy.

2The product can't pull users back (no daily trigger)

Problem: No notifications, no push, no automated reminders, online-only. Habit rides on personal discipline. This is the line between tool and operating system.

Solution — manufacture the daily trigger (Week 2–5):

  • Morning command-center: when the owner opens the app — "₹2.3L overdue · 3 quotes expiring today · 2 jobs due for dispatch · Machine #2 yield dropped 6%." One screen, every morning, the reason to open.
  • Automated WhatsApp/push reminders: overdue payments chase themselves; expiring quotes nudge the dealer; jobs-due alert ops. Turn our manual radars into automation.
  • Weekly digest (auto WhatsApp/email): revenue, collected, recovery rate, top customers — re-engages even on quiet weeks.
  • This is the single highest-leverage, lowest-cost build. Do it before anything heavy.

3We leak users at the compliance moment

Problem: At GST-filing time — the highest-intent moment of the month — we push users OUT to Tally/CA. No e-invoice (IRN/QR), no GSTR export, no credit notes (a legal gap), no accounting handoff. This is the #1 churn trigger as customers scale past the e-invoice threshold.

Solution — own the system of record (Month 2–3):

  • GST e-invoicing (IRN + QR) via a GSP/IRP integration — table-stakes above ₹5 Cr turnover. Without it we cap at micro-customers.
  • GSTR-1 / GSTR-3B export + Tally / Zoho Books export. Don't build accounting — integrate so the CA never pulls them off us.
  • Credit / debit notes + returns — close the legal gap and a daily workaround.
  • Once every invoice, ledger entry, and GST return lives here, switching cost becomes enormous. This is our real moat: data gravity.

4Only 1.5 of 4 seats are alive

Problem: Owner & dealer love it; ops treats it as a logbook; procurement churned in Month 1 (no PO/GRN). A "business OS" with two dead seats isn't an OS.

Solution — sequence the seats, don't boil the ocean:

5We charge nobody (no revenue engine)

Problem: No pricing, no billing, no free→paid loop. Investor GTM score 2.5. You can't be a 10 with ₹0 revenue.

Solution — turn it on now (Week 2–4):

Plus two non-subscription revenue lines later: payment-gateway take-rate on collected ₹, and a Slab Bazaar marketplace transaction fee (the network play already in our docs). Anchor every price against the value: "Recovery Radar saved you ₹3L of stone — Growth costs ₹30K/yr."

6 · CEO discipline — what we will NOT do

  • We will not chase horizontal ERP breadth. Integrate (Tally) where we're not best; own the stone workflow where we are.
  • We will not sell to enterprise yet. We win SMB stone businesses first; enterprise is a Year-2 arc.
  • We will not ship 20 features at once. Sequence: get-found → get-sticky → become-the-record → widen-seats. In that order.
  • We will not raise on a deck. We raise on 100 paying logos + an ROI case study. Traction is the only pitch that gets a 10.
  • We will not build accounting/books. The CA keeps Tally; we feed it. That's a feature, not a competitor.

7 · The honest answer about "10/10"

No software scores a literal 10 — and any advisor who promises it is selling you something. But here's the truth: our product can reach a 9 in ~6 months with the moves above, because the gaps are known, bounded, and mostly in our existing stack. The 10th point is not a feature — it's proof at scale: hundreds of stone businesses who would be genuinely unable to operate without us. We can't code that. We earn it — by getting found, becoming indispensable, charging fairly, and letting a tight trade refer its own. Do the sequence. The score follows.
