GraniteOS — 12-Month User Simulation

Not an analyst's review. Four real users adopt GraniteOS, live with it for a year, and we watch whether it becomes habit, dependency, and an operating system — or quietly dies on the home screen.

Companion to stakeholder-panel-review.html · Grounded in the actual build at D:\vyaparwerk\graniteos · Simulated 2026-06-10

The single fact that shapes every month below: GraniteOS today has no notifications, no push, no email/SMS, no automated reminders, and is online-only. Every "reminder" (udhaar, follow-up) is a manual WhatsApp message the user must choose to send. That means the platform cannot pull a user back in — there is no daily trigger the app itself fires. Habit therefore has to form from the user's own routine ("I check my money every morning"), not from the product nudging them. This is the difference between a tool people open when they have a task and an operating system they can't start the day without. I simulate that honestly — including the users who drift away because nothing reminds them to come back.

User 1 · Rajesh — Manufacturing Business Owner

Runs a granite factory + sales in Hyderabad. ~₹6 Cr/yr. Lives on his phone, books in Tally via his CA, chases payments by calling people.

Month 1 — Onboarding

Rajesh: "My nephew set it up in an evening. The setup asked my GST number, I imported my customer list from Excel. First time I saw a screen that knew what a slab and a godown is — not some American software pretending. I made a quote, it built the GST split itself, I WhatsApp'd it with a UPI link. Customer paid in two hours. That sold me."

Why adopt?Stone-native. The GST invoice + WhatsApp/UPI quote replaced a 20-minute manual job.
What excites?Recovery Radar — first time he could see Machine #2 wastes more stone. And Udhaar Radar showing exactly who owes him.
What confuses?Block→slab data entry is tedious upfront. "GST Auto-Pilot" input-ITC at flat 5% — he isn't sure if it matches his CA's number.
First win?A quote-to-payment in hours via UPI link. Tangible, day-one.
What prevents daily use?Nothing pings him. He has to remember to open it. His books still live in Tally, so he double-checks there.

Churn Simulation

Owner — Rajesh · churn scenario

Month 8: turnover crosses the e-invoice threshold. GraniteOS can't generate IRN/QR e-invoices and can't export to Tally. His CA demands proper e-invoices; he starts billing from a Tally add-on "just for compliance," then realises he's now invoicing in two places. Within two months he stops invoicing in GraniteOS and keeps it only for Recovery Radar — then forgets it.

Cause: compliance gap at the exact moment he scaled. Disappointing feature: GST Auto-Pilot looked like compliance but wasn't filing-grade. Unsupported workflow: e-invoice + accounting handoff. Competitor considered: Zoho Books / Vyapar / a Tally add-on.

Ops — Suresh · churn scenario

Month 5: a rush season hits. He needs to plan which job runs on which machine when. The board can't schedule, so he goes back to the whiteboard for planning and updates the app less and less, until the board goes stale and others stop trusting it.

Cause: it records but doesn't plan. Disappointing feature: fabrication board with no scheduling/capacity. Unsupported workflow: production planning + costing. Competitor: a dedicated job-shop/MES tool, or just the whiteboard.

Procurement — Anil · churn scenario (already churned)

Month 1: looks for POs, finds none, never returns. He never adopted, so there's nothing to lose.

Cause: core role unsupported. Disappointing feature: "supplier" being CRM, not procurement. Unsupported workflow: procure-to-pay. Competitor: Excel, or Zoho Inventory/Books, or any ERP with PO.

Dealer — Meena · churn scenario

Month 7: she's losing deals because open quotes go cold — nothing reminds her to follow up, and her sub-dealers keep phoning orders because there's no portal. A rival showroom tool offers auto follow-ups + a dealer ordering app. She trials it for the follow-up automation and her catalog motion gradually migrates.

Cause: no follow-up automation / no dealer portal. Disappointing feature: manual-only reminders. Unsupported workflow: lead nurture + B2B ordering. Competitor: a CRM-led showroom/dealer app.

The three founder answers

What would make users open it every day? A daily trigger the product fires itself — a morning digest + push/WhatsApp on overdue money, expiring quotes, and jobs due. Today nothing pulls them back; that's the biggest single gap between "tool" and "OS."

What would make them recommend it to peers? They already do, for the recovery radar + GST/UPI quoting + catalog. Referral is the strongest growth asset — and it's free, peer-to-peer in a tight trade. Amplify it with an in-app "share to a fellow stone owner."

What's required to become mission-critical? Become the system of record for money (e-invoice + returns + Tally export so they never leave for compliance), add the daily trigger (notifications/digest), and widen from 1.5 seats to 4 (procurement + ops planning + dealer portal). Then losing it = unable to run the business.

Final verdict — tool, platform, or operating system?

Today: a very good tool, evolving into a platform — not yet an operating system.

It's a tool because it does a few jobs (quote, invoice, collect, catalog, recovery) excellently and is opened when those jobs arise. It's becoming a platform for the owner and dealer, who run their sell-side and money on it and would feel real loss without it. It is not yet an operating system because (1) it doesn't fire its own daily trigger, so it can't make itself indispensable; (2) two of four core seats (procurement, ops-planning) live outside it; and (3) at the highest-intent moment — GST filing — it pushes users out to Tally instead of holding them.

Close the compliance loop, add notifications/digest, and light up procurement + production planning + a dealer portal, and GraniteOS crosses from "the app I bill from" to "the system I run my stone business on." The wedge and the love are already there — what's missing is the gravity to keep all four users inside it every day.
