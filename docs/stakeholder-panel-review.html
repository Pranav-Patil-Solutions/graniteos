GraniteOS — Industry Stakeholder Panel & Investor Review

A 15-seat board of manufacturing operators, channel partners, ERP evaluators, and a PE investor stress-test GraniteOS against the standard set in the brief: a unified operating system for the manufacturing ecosystem.

Target: graniteos.vercel.app · Evaluated 2026-06-10 · Method: grounded in the live deployment and the actual source at D:\vyaparwerk\graniteos

Read this first — two framing facts that decide everything below.
  1. The public URL is a login wall. A first-time visitor (and any web crawler, including an AI evaluator) reaches a marble sign-in screen and goes no further. There is no public landing page, value proposition, feature list, pricing, customer logos, or self-serve demo. By the brief's own rule — "do not assume features exist unless clearly demonstrated on the website" — almost everything would score Not Addressed and the Customer Journey Audit would collapse to "100% drop-off at login." That is itself the single most important finding.
  2. It is a vertical stone-trade SaaS, not a horizontal manufacturing ERP. The brief describes "ERP + CRM + procurement + inventory + sales + supplier + showroom + analytics + BPM." GraniteOS genuinely covers a slice of that — for the granite/marble/quartz trade in India specifically. Judged as "a unified manufacturing OS" it is incomplete; judged as "the operating system for an Indian stone fabricator/dealer" it is a focused, surprisingly mature MVP. I evaluate it as both, and say which lens I'm using.
To give you a real answer instead of 200 rows of "can't tell," I evaluated the working product behind the login by reading the source. So scores reflect what is actually built, with a separate, harsh public-facing audit in Part 8.

What is actually built (ground truth)

Working today:

  • Multi-tenant SaaS, Postgres RLS company-scoping, 4 roles, team invites
  • Inventory: blocks → slabs, recovery-rate (sqft/CBM) radar, 3D slab viewer, public slab "passport" + catalog share links
  • CRM: customers + suppliers, GSTIN, credit limit, opening balance, udhaar
  • Quote → Order → Invoice → Payment pipeline
  • GST Rule-46 tax invoice: CGST/SGST/IGST, per-line HSN, multi-rate, GSTIN check-digit validation, FY numbering, amount-in-words
  • Fabrication board: job stages queued→cutting→polishing→edging→QC→ready→dispatched
  • Analytics: revenue, collections, receivables, stock value, 6-mo trend, quote funnel, top customers
  • "GST Auto-Pilot" (output GST − input ITC), "Udhaar Radar" WhatsApp reminders
  • AI marketing copy (Groq) in English/Hinglish/Hindi · Excel bulk import · product master

Absent (claimed-ERP gaps):

  • Payment gateway (Razorpay/PayU) — manual entry only
  • E-invoicing (GSTN IRP / IRN+QR) and E-way bill
  • GSTR-1 / GSTR-3B return exports · Tally/accounting export
  • Credit/debit notes & sales returns (a GST legal requirement)
  • Procurement: no purchase-order / GRN / inward-receipt UI (prefix reserved, table absent)
  • Multi-location / branch / warehouse (single company; godown is free text)
  • Production costing — no labour/machine/time/cost capture; stage-only
  • Forecasting, budgeting, per-job profitability
  • WhatsApp Business API, SMS, email (only manual share links)
  • Public API, SSO/SAML, audit log, SOC2/ISO, SLA, uptime page
  • Public marketing site, pricing, demo, trust signals

Stack: Next.js 15 / React 19, Supabase (Postgres + Auth, 14 migrations, RLS + SECURITY DEFINER RPCs), Tailwind, Three.js, Groq/Vercel AI SDK. Tests: Playwright e2e (auth) + Vitest units (GST, validation, permissions). Maturity: solid MVP / Slice-1 foundation — pilot-ready for 1–10 companies, not market-ready.

Part 10 · Executive Summary

What it does exceptionally well

  • Recovery-Rate Radar — a stone-native metric no horizontal ERP touches. Real moat.
  • GST Rule-46 invoicing — genuinely production-grade (HSN per line, CGST/SGST/IGST, place of supply, FY numbering, amount in words).
  • Sell-side loop — quote→order→invoice→payment, clean and complete.
  • Slab Passport + 3D + public catalog — a buyer experience competitors don't ship.
  • India-SMB fit — udhaar, WhatsApp/UPI, Hindi AI copy, mobile-first, day-one setup.

Biggest weaknesses

  • No public shopfront — login wall = no acquisition.
  • Finance stack incomplete — no e-invoice, GST returns, credit notes, or accounting export.
  • No procurement — PO/GRN/3-way-match absent despite the claim.
  • Shallow manufacturing — no scheduling, costing, BOM, or consumption.
  • No monetisation — no pricing/billing; no traction shown.

Fastest wins for conversionsPublic landing + pricing + "Get a Demo" funnel; expose the /showcase demo & recovery-radar story; add trust signals.

Final verdict

Is this a complete manufacturing operating system? No — not as a horizontal manufacturing ERP, and not even a complete stone OS yet (procurement, costing, accounting, multi-location missing). It is an excellent Slice-1 sell-side + GST + inventory MVP for the Indian stone trade, with a real wedge.

Who should buy today? Small-to-mid granite/marble dealers, showrooms, and fabricators in India who live on quotes, slab inventory, udhaar collection, and GST invoices — and who keep books in Tally/with a CA. For them the recovery radar, slab passport, and one-tap GST invoice are immediately worth paying for.

Who should NOT buy today? Anyone needing procurement/PO, full accounting, e-invoicing at scale, multi-branch, production costing/scheduling, or enterprise compliance. Pure quarries/suppliers (no sell-side flow) and non-stone manufacturers are out of scope.
